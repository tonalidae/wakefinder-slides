Data analysis

  • N-body simulations run with Gadget-3 (Garavito-Camargo et al. 2021), DM mass resolution 4×104 M☉
  • 108 particles are needed to capture resonances; we analyse 106 of them, beyond 50 kpc from the halo centre
  • 2 datasets — the same snapshot (t = 1.1 Gyr) from two independent runs:
    1. Unperturbed halo, no LMC
    2. Halo perturbed by the LMC
  • Not a before/after in time — the two runs are matched particle by particle through a shared id
  • Data: 3D positions and velocities with their id
  • Histograms: position, and angular momentum computed from the particle velocities and their id.
  • Classification into low and high angular momentum regions

Physical intuition: does angular momentum spatially delimit the wake?
\[\begin{aligned} \vec{L} = \vec{r} \times \vec{v} \end{aligned} \]
How the wake is selected

Cut 1 — in the unperturbed halo, keep L < 10 000 kpc km/s

Cut 2 — take those same ids in the perturbed halo and keep the band L1 < L < L2, with r < 125 kpc

\[\begin{aligned} L_1 &= 220\,(r-50) + 13\,000 \\ L_2 &= 220\,(r-50) + 25\,000 \end{aligned}\]

The 125 kpc limit follows a spike in the distance distribution, followed by a drop in |L|.

What the phase diagram shows
  • Selected particles pile up at positive Lz: they corotate with the LMC, whose orbit lies in the XY plane
  • Energy rises by about 10 000 — the halo expands, as seen in galaxy mergers
  • The sharp edge in the red distribution is our own L = 10 000 selection threshold
  • No pre-interaction data needed — unlike selection by angular momentum increments alone, so the method can be applied to real observations
